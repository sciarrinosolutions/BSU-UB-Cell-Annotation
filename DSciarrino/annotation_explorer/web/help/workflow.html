Workflow panel

The bottom-right panel maps papers onto the steps of a scRNA-seq analysis. It has two views, switched with the toggle above the diagram: This paper and All ingested papers.

The workflow steps

StepCovers
scRNA-seqThe sequencing technology or protocol (e.g. 10x Chromium, CITE-seq, Smart-seq2).
QuantificationTurning reads into a count matrix (e.g. Cell Ranger, Alevin).
Expression matrix & preprocessingQC filtering, doublets, normalization, highly variable genes, differential expression / marker selection.
Dimensionality reductionPCA, UMAP, t-SNE, learned embeddings.
Batch correctionIntegrating samples or datasets (e.g. Seurat anchors, Harmony, scVI).
ClusteringGrouping cells (e.g. SNN graph clustering, Louvain, Leiden).
AnnotationAssigning cell type labels; the project's main focus.
TrajectoryOrdering cells along differentiation or time.
Cell–cell communicationLigand–receptor interaction analysis.

"This paper" view

Each step is colored by how the selected paper relates to it:

LabelMeaning
Core contribution (dark)The paper's main contribution is at this step.
Used (light)The paper performs this step with existing methods.
Mentioned (dashed outline)Discussed, assumed or only shown, not performed.
Not addressed (faint)The paper doesn't cover this step.
Unknown (dotted)The agent couldn't tell from what it read, usually because only the abstract was available.

Click a step (or Tab to it and press Enter) to see a short description of what the paper does there, plus the methods and tools it names, and every algorithm and tool in the paper with its role: proposed (the paper's own), used, or compared (a baseline).

Tool and method names with a highlight on hover are clickable: they open that tool's card in the "All ingested papers" view.

"All ingested papers" view

The same diagram, now summarizing every ingested paper:

  • Shading: the darker a step, the larger the share of papers that perform it (core or used).
  • Box label: e.g. 9/13 papers · Seurat means 9 of 13 papers perform the step and Seurat is its most-used tool. Papers marked unknown for a step are left out of that step's total.

Click a step to see ranked bar charts of its tools and methods (top 12 each). Hover a bar for a breakdown by role; click it to open the tool card. A * after a name means it isn't in the vocabulary yet (vocabulary).

How usage is counted

Choose the measure in the drop-down:

MeasureCountsAnswers
Papers using itPapers that use or propose the tool at this stepWhat do people actually run?
Used as a baselinePapers that compare against itWhat do new methods benchmark against?
Proposed herePapers where it's the paper's own methodWhich new methods target this step?
Citation-weightedSum of the citation scores (0–1) of papers using itWhat do the most-cited papers use?
  • Each paper counts once per tool per step, however often the tool is mentioned.
  • Mentioned-only uses aren't counted under any measure, so a tool someone only names doesn't get credit as if they ran it.
  • Name variants count as one tool: Seurat, Seurat v4 and Seurat v5 are all Seurat. Versions are shown on the tool card.
  • Preferred tier only restricts every count (and the coverage numbers) to papers in the preferred tier.

The counts describe the papers ingested so far, which lean toward annotation-method papers. Treat them as a demonstration until many more papers are ingested; see limitations.

Tool cards

A tool card summarizes one tool or method across all ingested papers:

  • Its kind (toolkit, database, LLM…), language, the versions seen and a link.
  • By workflow step: how many papers use it at each step. Click a step to jump to its chart.
  • Papers: every paper that uses it and in what role. Click one to open it.
  • Show only these papers in the list: filters the paper list to them. Clear it with the × on the chip above the list.
